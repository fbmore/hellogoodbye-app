HelloGoodbye

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026 · applies to HelloGoodbye 2.0 and later for iPhone, iPad and Mac

HelloGoodbye helps you write messages faster. Your templates, recipients and Outbox stay on your device, and messages leave it only when you send them through Mail, Messages, WhatsApp or another app you choose.

In short  No account. No tracking. No analytics. No ads. We have no servers, so we never receive your messages, your contacts or anything else you put in the app.

Who we are

HelloGoodbye is made by FRANCESCO BERTOCCI, LLC (“we”). Questions about this policy: fbmore+hellogoodbye@gmail.com.

What the app stores, and where

Everything below is stored only on your device, in a private folder shared by the HelloGoodbye app and its share extension (the App Group group.com.fbmore.HelloGoodbye). It is included in your device backups like any other app data. HelloGoodbye does not sync it through iCloud or any other service, and we cannot access it.

How messages are sent

HelloGoodbye never sends anything by itself. When you tap Send, the finished message is handed to:

  • Mail (or your default email app on the Mac) for email,
  • Messages for text messages,
  • WhatsApp for WhatsApp messages — the WhatsApp app if it is installed, otherwise a wa.me link in your browser, which passes the phone number and message text to WhatsApp,
  • or the app you pick from the share sheet.

You see the message there and confirm it yourself. From that point it is handled by that app and its own privacy policy (for example Apple’s or WhatsApp’s). We never receive a copy.

When a message includes files and goes to WhatsApp through the share sheet, HelloGoodbye also copies its text to the clipboard so you can paste it into the chat.

Network use

HelloGoodbye contacts no server of ours, because there isn’t one. The only request the app makes itself is a connectivity check: while it believes you are offline, it asks Apple’s standard captive.apple.com page whether the internet is reachable. That request carries no information about you or your messages.

Permissions

  • Contacts: HelloGoodbye does not ask for access to your contacts. When you tap the contact button, iOS shows its own picker, and only the email address or phone number you choose is given to the app.
  • Notifications (optional): used to tell you when you are back online with messages waiting, or that a message shared from another app is ready to finish in HelloGoodbye. Notifications are created on your device; you can turn them off in Settings → Notifications → HelloGoodbye.
  • Files: only the files you pick in Settings or share to HelloGoodbye are read.
  • Background refresh: if messages are waiting for a connection, iOS may briefly wake HelloGoodbye once you are online, to show the back-online notification.

Notifications show who a waiting message is for. With the iOS default “Show Previews: When Unlocked”, that text isn’t visible on the lock screen.

What we do not collect

  • No account, sign-in or email registration
  • No analytics, usage statistics or crash reporting of our own
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers
  • No tracking across apps or websites
  • No location, no contacts database, no photos library access
  • No third-party software development kits

If you choose to share diagnostics with Apple in your device settings, Apple may share crash reports with developers; those are covered by Apple’s privacy policy.

Contacting us

If you email us (for example with “Send Feedback” in the app), we receive your email address and whatever you write, and use them only to reply. We don’t add you to any list.

Deleting your data

Remove templates, lists, attachments and Outbox messages inside the app, or delete HelloGoodbye to remove everything it stored. Messages you already sent live in Mail, Messages or WhatsApp and are deleted there.

Children

HelloGoodbye is not directed at children under 13 and collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date at the top. Material changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes.
